# Tracking planner contract review

**No concrete implementation defect was found that explains the current-gain or true-state reference losing to public nominal control.** The checked code implements its declared approximate, finite-search controller. Those references are privileged inputs to that controller, not optimal-control bounds. Several real objective/model mismatches remain; this review does not establish which caused the observed ordering.

This is a read-only diagnosis of the completed single seed410 engineering case. No simulator, planner, model, RNG, training or new benchmark was run. All 14 live sources and snapshots were rehashed against the completed run; the six saved audit records and six native episode records were authenticated against the prior complete review. Source references below identify the exact lines read; no source was changed.

## What the completed evidence establishes

The saved audit checked 1,200 actual transitions, 2,987,520 candidate transitions, 1,000 selected re-advances and 5,040 identification transitions. Actual/selected-state and identifier maximum errors were zero; every saved candidate angle matched independent native replay exactly. The largest independent NumPy-versus-Torch geometry discrepancy was 2.384185791015625e-7. These are authenticated **existing** audit findings, not a replay performed here.

The saved arithmetic review gives total native costs: nominal 22.69959452, public current-gain 26.78943054, true-state/current-gain 28.50878979. For POST [80,200), their per-action costs are 0.10459064, 0.10280964 and 0.12520961 respectively. Thus the current-gain reference actually improves this post-change interval by 1.7028% versus nominal, while its complete episode is worse. Neither observation supports a general ordering across seeds.

## Strongest remaining objective mismatch

Candidate physics propagates the issued command without any disturbance, while only the action penalty integrates the known noise distribution. The analytic cost is exactly E[||clip(command+epsilon)||²], `reacher_reward_residual.py:32-86`; candidate transitions instead set ctrl directly to the issued command, `reacher_geometry_physics.py:225-240`. Consequently the controller does not optimize expected noisy fingertip distance. Even propagating the mean clipped action would not generally equal expected nonlinear future state/distance. Near saturation, the issued command also differs from mean clipped applied action.

In the actual plant, the same gain multiplies useful action and disturbance torque. Before clipping, sigma0.05 corresponds to a torque disturbance scale7 at gain0.7 and13 at gain1.3. Nominal candidate physics omits both. This is an explicit certainty-equivalent approximation shared by all roles, with gain-dependent consequences. It is a plausible mechanism for an accurate-parameter controller failing to win; it is **not established as the cause** by source inspection or these saved six trajectories.

Finite-budget search adds another limitation: identical initial innovations do not imply identical later CEM banks. Scores determine subsequent proposals, and controller actions create different later roots. A more accurate state/gain model can therefore produce a different search trajectory and closed-loop policy, without a guaranteed improvement. Public velocity reconstruction may act as a biased filter, but that is a hypothesis; these records do not prove beneficial smoothing or regularization.

The immediate conclusion is to retain the engineering result and inspect the already-saved candidate-quality evidence before choosing any changed scoring/noise/search contract. Do not relabel public nominal as a better state estimator, treat true-state as an optimal oracle, lower a qualification threshold, or infer architectural opportunity from this one ordering. A changed objective or stochastic planner would be a new prospective comparison, not a correction justified by a discovered bug.

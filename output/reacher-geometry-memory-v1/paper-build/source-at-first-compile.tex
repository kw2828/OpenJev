{\LARGE\bfseries Persistent Memory with Fixed Geometry Scoring}\[5pt]
{\large An OpenJev Reacher development study}\[5pt]
OpenJev project\quad $\cdot$\quad September 19, 2026 (UTC)\[3pt]
{\small Development report; not an ICLR submission or an independently replicated result.}
\end{center}

\textbf{Abstract.} We evaluate whether persistent learned state remains useful when
Reacher controllers share a fixed geometry-based scoring rule. Twelve inherited
models, comprising three paired fits of four learned families, are evaluated on
64 fresh paired cases in each of three sensing conditions. No models are fitted
in this study. Persistent GRU reduces mean native episode cost by 31.86\% under
six-step sensing gaps and 25.99\% under ten-step gaps relative to a GRU that
resets its hidden state and retains the last observed angles. All 25 frozen
continuation checks pass. Supplied-physics controllers still outperform every
learned fit. The result supports these trained persistent update policies over
the declared reset controls in this task; it does not establish a novel
architecture, biological mechanism, calibrated uncertainty or general robotics
capability. A trained two-valid-observation/action-history control remains unresolved.

\section*{1\quad Comparison and task}
The environment is Gymnasium Reacher-v5 with native reward weights 1 for
distance and control cost, 50 actions per episode and $\Delta t=0.02$ seconds.
An issued command $a_t\in[-1,1]^2$ receives independent hidden Gaussian actuator
noise with standard deviation 0.05 and is clipped again before execution.
Reported utility is native episode cost $C=-\sum_{t=0}^{49}r_t$, so lower is better.
The learned controller sees
\[
 p_t=[\cos q_0,\cos q_1,\sin q_0,\sin q_1,g_x,g_y,\mathrm{valid},\mathrm{age}],
\]
with unavailable angle entries masked to zero. Native velocity, realized noise
and fingertip displacement are excluded. The public target remains known.
Conditions are full sensing and two gaps of length six or ten; gap onset phase
varies by case. Full sensing is still partially observed because velocity is withheld.

The GRU families share the same 36,805-parameter schema and paired original
initialization, training corpus, order and update count, but their final weights
were trained separately. Persistent GRU carries learned state across real
observations and actions. Current-packet GRU resets and encodes the actual
packet; cached-observation GRU resets and encodes the last measured angles
with current goal, validity and age. Cached MLP has 36,599 parameters and the
same public angle cache. All GRUs remain recurrent in imagined rollouts.
The treatment includes the trained real-observation update policy and validity
gating, rather than recurrence in isolation.

\section*{2\quad All fits retained; persistent update policies improve cost}
\begin{center}

\captionof{figure}{All 36 learned fit/panel points and 15 reference points.

Against current-packet GRU, persistent GRU lowers mean cost by 38.22\% and
35.36\% on the two gap panels. Against cached-observation GRU, the corresponding
reductions are 31.86\% and 25.99\%; all three paired fits improve in both panels.
The full-sensing reductions are 31.07\% and 29.93\%, respectively, so the benefit
is not confined to missing measurements. This experiment does not identify
velocity inference as the mechanism.

For persistent minus cached GRU, the saved conditional paired-case 95\%
cost-difference intervals are $[-3.1291,-2.2102]$ for six-step gaps and
$[-2.5642,-1.8672]$ for ten-step gaps. These intervals hold the three fits and
training corpus fixed; they are neither training-seed population uncertainty
nor multiplicity-corrected confirmation. No new resampling was performed for this report.

Persistent GRU also improves over cached MLP by 18.15\% and 12.96\% on the gap
panels, a secondary result that cannot rescue the primary rule. All three
supplied-physics references are better than every learned fit in every panel.
Persistent cost is 23.04\% and 32.40\% higher than public-kinematic physics on
the gap panels. These physics references use supplied nominal dynamics;
known-state physics additionally receives current true position and velocity.
They are not optimal stochastic-control oracles.
\end{landscape}

\section*{3\quad Fixed scoring and candidate budgets, unequal total computation}
\begin{center}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{evidence/reacher-geometry-memory-v1/report/utility-vs-cost.pdf}
\captionof{figure}{All learned fits and references, with no selected frontier.
The horizontal coordinate is complete recorded row wall time divided by

not isolated action latency or a cross-machine speed result.}

Every nonfloor controller uses geometry scoring and CEM256 with horizon 12,
three-action blocks, a paid mean candidate, global-best retention and terminal
truncation. Geometry projects predicted trigonometric features with $\mathrm{atan2}$,
uses 0.10/0.11\,m link offsets, and subtracts expected clipped actuator cost once.
Predicted per-step rewards are clipped to $[-2.5,0]$ for planning. Original
learned reward heads still execute and are charged. Native episode cost is
reported without replacing it by this planning score.

Reset states, hidden disturbances and planner innovations are paired across
rows; score-adaptive CEM proposals can subsequently differ. Equal candidate
budgets do not match information or total compute. Persistent rows take
20.49--22.85\,s; other learned rows, 21.76--24.62\,s; supplied-physics rows,
138.31--143.96\,s. These times include controller setup, native stepping and
trace storage. Global source/checkpoint validation and copying are separate
execution costs. The complete execution takes 2,109.20\,s within its frozen
5,700\,s cap; audit takes 1,384.44\,s within its separate 3,300\,s cap.
\end{landscape}

\section*{4\quad Frozen decision rule and verification boundary}
The study retains all twelve inherited fits, all 51 control rows and all 64
cases per row. There are zero new fits, optimizer updates or Astra calls.
The frozen rule requires persistent GRU to improve both reset-GRU family
means by at least 3\% on both gap panels, be no worse in any paired fit, and
degrade by no more than 2\% on full sensing. Persistent fits on both gap panels
and known-state physics on ordinary gaps must each beat zero action by at
least 10\%. All \textbf{25/25 checks pass} without threshold changes.

The saved-output auditor authenticates the 90 frozen sources, checkpoint

replays each have maximum absolute discrepancy zero. The audit makes
\textbf{zero learned-model calls}: saved neural predictions and hidden states

Equal before/after weights alone do not prove the absence of intermediate
updates. Publication uses saved artifacts only and adds no scientific calls.

\section*{5\quad Interpretation and next control}
This is fresh-case evaluation of previously trained models in a repeatedly
studied simulator, not an independent training replication. The common geometry
score approximates native RK4 cached-body reward; distance at projected mean
angles is not expected distance. Neither the physics advantage nor the learned
gap quantifies optimal control. Training data and paired initialization reduce
some confounds, but differing trained update rules, validity gates and final
weights prevent attributing the result to recurrence alone.

The next conventional control is a parameter-identical GRU trained to rebuild
state from its last two valid observations and all intervening issued actions.
That trained comparison is unresolved. Component tests do not supply an
effectiveness result. A separate protocol, complete runner/audit, rehearsal
and capacity measurement are required before fitting or evaluation. A second
environment and fresh training replication remain separate evidence needs.
No connectome or biological advantage, Bayesian inference, calibrated
uncertainty, architectural novelty or general robotics performance is established.

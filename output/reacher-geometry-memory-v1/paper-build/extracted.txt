Persistent Memory with Fixed Geometry Scoring
An OpenJev Reacher development study
OpenJev project·September 19, 2026 (UTC)
Development report; not an ICLR submission or an independently replicated result.
Abstract.We evaluate whether persistent learned state remains useful when Reacher controllers share a fixed geometry-
based scoring rule. Twelve inherited models, comprising three paired fits of four learned families, are evaluated on 64
fresh paired cases in each of three sensing conditions. No models are fitted in this study. Persistent GRU reduces mean
native episode cost by 31.86% under six-step sensing gaps and 25.99% under ten-step gaps relative to a GRU that resets
its hidden state and retains the last observed angles. All 25 frozen continuation checks pass. Supplied-physics controllers
still outperform every learned fit. The result supports these trained persistent update policies over the declared reset
controls in this task; it does not establish a novel architecture, biological mechanism, calibrated uncertainty or general
robotics capability. A trained two-valid-observation/action-history control remains unresolved.

Against current-packet GRU, persistent GRU lowers mean cost by 38.22% and 35.36% on the two gap panels. Against cached-observation GRU, the corresponding
reductions are 31.86% and 25.99%; all three paired fits improve in both panels. The full-sensing reductions are 31.07% and 29.93%, respectively, so the benefit
is not confined to missing measurements. This experiment does not identify velocity inference as the mechanism.
For persistent minus cached GRU, the saved conditional paired-case 95% cost-difference intervals are[−3.1291,−2.2102]for six-step gaps and[−2.5642,−1.8672]
for ten-step gaps. These intervals hold the three fits and training corpus fixed; they are neither training-seed population uncertainty nor multiplicity-corrected
confirmation. No new resampling was performed for this report.
Persistent GRU also improves over cached MLP by 18.15% and 12.96% on the gap panels, a secondary result that cannot rescue the primary rule. All three
supplied-physics references are better than every learned fit in every panel. Persistent cost is 23.04% and 32.40% higher than public-kinematic physics on the
gap panels. These physics references use supplied nominal dynamics; known-state physics additionally receives current true position and velocity. They are not
optimal stochastic-control oracles.

Every nonfloor controller uses geometry scoring and CEM256 with horizon 12, three-action blocks, a paid mean candidate, global-best retention and terminal
truncation. Geometry projects predicted trigonometric features withatan2, uses 0.10/0.11m link offsets, and subtracts expected clipped actuator cost once.
Predicted per-step rewards are clipped to[−2.5,0]for planning. Original learned reward heads still execute and are charged. Native episode cost is reported
without replacing it by this planning score.
Reset states, hidden disturbances and planner innovations are paired across rows; score-adaptive CEM proposals can subsequently differ. Equal candidate budgets
do not match information or total compute. Persistent rows take 20.49 to 22.85s; other learned rows, 21.76 to 24.62s; supplied-physics rows, 138.31 to 143.96s.

complete execution takes 2,109.20s within its frozen 5,700s cap; audit takes 1,384.44s within its separate 3,300s cap.

4 Frozen decision rule and verification boundary
The study retains all twelve inherited fits, all 51 control rows and all 64 cases per row. There are zero new fits, optimizer
updates or Astra calls. The frozen rule requires persistent GRU to improve both reset-GRU family means by at least
3% on both gap panels, be no worse in any paired fit, and degrade by no more than 2% on full sensing. Persistent fits
on both gap panels and known-state physics on ordinary gaps must each beat zero action by at least 10%. All25/25
checks passwithout threshold changes.

5 Interpretation and next control
This is fresh-case evaluation of previously trained models in a repeatedly studied simulator, not an independent training
replication. The common geometry score approximates native RK4 cached-body reward; distance at projected mean
angles is not expected distance. Neither the physics advantage nor the learned gap quantifies optimal control. Training
data and paired initialization reduce some confounds, but differing trained update rules, validity gates and final weights
prevent attributing the result to recurrence alone.
The next conventional control is a parameter-identical GRU trained to rebuild state from its last two valid observations
andallinterveningissuedactions. Thattrainedcomparisonisunresolved. Componenttestsdonotsupplyaneffectiveness
result. A separate protocol, complete runner/audit, rehearsal and capacity measurement are required before fitting or
evaluation. A second environment and fresh training replication remain separate evidence needs. No connectome or
biological advantage, Bayesian inference, calibrated uncertainty, architectural novelty or general robotics performance
is established.

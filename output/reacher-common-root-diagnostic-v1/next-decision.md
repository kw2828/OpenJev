# Decision after the common-root diagnostic

**Stop this task/controller recipe as evidence for learned adaptation.** All three separately specified engineering contrasts failed their rules. The frozen decision tree therefore selects neither extra search nor longer-horizon follow-up for this recipe.

The longer horizon reduced average branch cost by 7.32699% and improved the mean on all three source trajectories, but only seven of twelve slots were nonworse; eight were required. Accurate gain reduced cost by 2.51046%, short of the required 3%. More short search increased average cost by 0.01769%. These facts may motivate understanding the planner, but they do not satisfy the declared continuation conditions.

All twelve slots and independent native audits completed in one attempt. All 30 bound sources remained unchanged. Ten distinct native roots appear in the twelve identity slots; repeated startup states and all their branch arrays are bit-identical. The original inputs, all failures from earlier studies and their thresholds remain preserved.

Do not add a third short restart, another horizon, a terminal reward, a favorable root subset, an updated threshold or learned adaptation to this same configuration. The earlier four-fresh-case qualification remains unlaunched, and its rules are unchanged. This closes a specific controller/task candidate, not the overall objective of finding useful novel architectures.

The next research direction must have a demonstrable role for information retained across time. Its first experiment should qualify the task against a strong conventional controller or estimator, a finite-history baseline and an explicit information reference before training a new recurrent or biological model. The current diagnostic provides no justification to claim that anatomical wiring, fast weights or a learned world model would solve the gap.

# After the conditional-label comparison

**The averaging recipe failed its continuation rule.** Mean decision regret
improved 4.89% in the base setting and worsened 6.15% in the shifted setting.
The independent audit agrees with every reported comparison. Keep this outcome
closed; it does not earn a larger confirmation run or an architectural claim.

The earlier expensive reference showed that useful decisions can be made by
averaging possible-future costs. This follow-up did not show that the same
benefit transfers to a small GRU through mean-target supervision. It also does
not isolate the cause: finite labels, optimization, representation and the
sensitivity of discrete action choices to cost-prediction errors remain distinct
questions. A failed averaging comparison is not evidence for a connectome or
world-model bottleneck.

The independently developed [observation-operator component](otto-observation-operator-status.md)
has passed internal probability and gradient checks. Before another empirical
architecture comparison, establish that its constrained state can learn
nontrivial decision moments and retain useful distinctions over long gaps on a
known finite-state problem. The current nearly uniform dense initialization can
mix states rapidly; mass conservation alone does not establish useful memory.
This is a proposed separate qualification, not a result or a registered run.

Any later native comparison must use a new protocol and data, match branch and
multihorizon supervision, and include both an ordinary GRU and the untied
observation-operator control. Endpoint-only labels do not identify an
observation decomposition. Count operator construction, normalization,
precision, parameter storage and all external feature computation. Existing
negative results remain part of that comparison's motivation and evidence.

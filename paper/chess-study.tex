\begin{abstract}
We specify a bounded study of synthetic signed recurrent circuits for chess move
selection. A structured circuit and a degree-preserving rewired control share
training data, initialization of common modules, optimizer, and internal update
count. A GRU provides a conventional baseline. Students distill fixed-node
Stockfish decisions from generated, game-separated positions. A separate arena
compares these students with published ChessFly and ChessLFM policies, an
unchanged OpenJev Qwen scorer, and a Codex-dispatched Astra player. These external
comparisons are heterogeneous demonstrations, not architectural ablations.
Across three seeds on 1,024 development positions, circuit, rewired and GRU
students achieve 14.3555\%, 14.1927\% and 14.5182\% mean teacher agreement.
The circuit's 0.16276-percentage-point gain over rewired fails the predeclared
3-point continuation threshold. \textbf{This is a negative topology-screening
result, not evidence of architectural superiority, Elo or a novel algorithm.}
The 24-position public puzzle panel and 18-game exhibition schedule are complete.
We propose a separate history-dependent
state-transition diagnostic and distinguish it from computation within a board
and biological connectome evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question and prior work}
Does a structured signed graph improve chess decisions over a rewired graph
when signed degrees, training data, and internal computation budget are held
fixed? This question is narrower than whether recurrent networks can play chess.
Designed sparse recurrent wiring is established in neural circuit
policies~\cite{lechner2020}; policy distillation is established~\cite{rusu2015};
and recurrent computation with additional inference steps has already been
studied on chess~\cite{schwarzschild2021}. Our cell uses simple discrete tanh
dynamics, not the continuous-time dynamics of neural circuit policies.
Published ChessFly and ChessLFM artifacts provide further direct
prior context~\cite{chessfly,chesslfm}. We do not claim novelty from combining
these ingredients or placing them behind an all-legal-move interface.

\section{Current student protocol}
\subsection{Observations, recurrence, and actions}
A student receives an 841-dimensional numeric representation of the current
FEN: 12 piece channels over 64 squares, side to move, castling rights,
en-passant square including an explicit absent category, and two move counters.
Teacher moves and scores never enter these features. A shared affine/tanh
encoder produces $z\in\mathbb{R}^{64}$. Each decision starts from
$h^{(0)}=0$ and applies four updates to the \emph{same board}.
The GRU update is $h^{(k+1)}=\operatorname{GRU}(z,h^{(k)})$.
For either circuit arm,
\begin{align}
 h_i^{(k+1)} &= \tanh\!\left(b_i+d_i z_i+
                    \sum_{j:(j,i)\in E} w_{ij}h_j^{(k)}\right),\\
 w_{ij} &= \frac{s_{ij}\operatorname{softplus}(a_{ij})}
                    {\sqrt{\max(1,\deg_{\mathrm{in}}(i))}},
 \qquad d_i=\mathbf{1}[i<16].
\end{align}
The structured graph has 16 sensory, 24 inter, 16 command, and 8 motor units,
with 352 edges. Sensory units project to inter units; inter units project to
command units; command units project to other command units and motor units.
Every fourth source unit is inhibitory. The control uses a fixed 20,000
double-edge-swap proposal budget and preserves each node's signed incoming and
outgoing degrees, with no self-edges or duplicates. There is currently one
structured graph and one rewired graph, not a graph-seed distribution.

The final hidden state feeds a 1,968-entry geometric UCI move head and a scalar
tanh value head. The move vocabulary includes promotions; a python-chess mask
retains \emph{all} legal moves before softmax. Thus legality is supplied by a
symbolic rules engine. The model is not being tested on learning chess rules.
The value head reads all hidden coordinates, as does the move head; motor-unit
labels do not imply a motor-only readout.

\paragraph{Capacity and implementation limits.}
The two circuit arms have 182,289 declared parameters each; the GRU has 206,833.
Shared encoder and output-head initialization match within a weight seed.
However, the circuits receive only 16 encoder coordinates whereas GRU receives
all 64. Consequently 48 encoder rows, totaling 40,416 parameters, do not affect
circuit outputs. The GRU comparison does not isolate either topology or input
capacity. The circuit implementation builds a dense $64\times64$ matrix for
each update; sparse parameterization is not evidence of sparse-kernel speed.

\subsection{Teacher data and optimization}
The protocol specifies 4,096 training and 1,024 development positions from
disjoint generated games. At each step, the generator chooses a uniformly
random legal move or the teacher move with equal probability. Each game has a
40-ply cap, with fixed generation caps of 160 training and 64 development games.
Game seeds are predetermined. Global state deduplication includes pieces, side,
castling and en-passant state, while ignoring halfmove/fullmove counters.
Reaching a generation cap without filling the quota is a recorded failure,
not a reason to extend the budget.

Stockfish 19~\cite{stockfish} labels positions with a 2,000-node request,
one thread, 16 MB hash, and cleared hash before analysis. Teacher analysis
receives FEN without preceding move history, matching the student's history-free
observation. Both labeling calls and teacher-selected rollout moves are charged
to teacher computation; requested and reported nodes are retained separately.
The target move is the first principal-variation move. The value target is
$y_v=\tanh(c/600)$ for side-to-move centipawns $c$, using signed 10,000 centipawns
for mate scores. This target is not a win probability.

Each of the three modes uses seeds 17, 29 and 43. Data, epoch permutations
within seed, and training budget match across modes. Training uses three epochs,
batch size 64, Adam with learning rate $10^{-3}$, betas $(0.9,0.999)$,
$\epsilon=10^{-8}$, no weight decay, and gradient norm clipping at 1.
The loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{L}=-\log\pi_\theta(a^*\mid x,\mathcal{A}_{\rm legal}(x))
                 +0.5\bigl(v_\theta(x)-y_v\bigr)^2.
\end{equation}
This gives 192 optimizer updates per fit and nine final fits. Runs use two CPU
threads, randomized fit order, and no early stopping, checkpoint selection or
replacement fits. This is supervised distillation, not reinforcement learning.

\subsection{Evaluation and evidence}
All final checkpoints use the same generated development set. Report per-seed
and aggregate teacher agreement, target negative log likelihood, value MAE and
MSE, parameters, and training/evaluation time. Reference baselines are the exact
uniform-legal expected agreement and the most frequent training target among
legal moves. Teacher agreement measures imitation of a bounded engine, not
optimal move quality. Positions from a game are correlated; position count is
not an independent-sample count.

Plans bind source, dependency and engine hashes. Data receipts retain generated
games, FENs, analyses, teacher costs, and split membership. Fit receipts bind
checkpoints, logs, predictions and fixed update counts. These checks establish
execution identity, not efficacy. All nine supervised fits completed.
Table~\ref{tab:results} reports the saved development results; these positions
are not an untouched confirmation panel.

The frozen descriptive gate requires at least a 3-point circuit mean agreement
gain over rewired; at most a 3-point mean deficit to GRU; no paired seed more
than 5 points below rewired; and at most 0.03 additional mean value MAE.
The first check fails: observed gain is 0.16276 points. The other three pass,
so the conjunction fails. We retain that stop decision instead of extending
the run or promoting a topology claim.

\section{Separate public-player arena}
The separately fixed 18-game schedule uses a color-paired round robin among
the circuit student, published ChessFly, ChessLFM and Astra through Codex,
plus circuit pairs against OpenJev Qwen, GRU and rewired students.
Student seed 17 and the first scheduled game's replay were selected before
training outcomes, without selecting a winning checkpoint or replay.
All 18 scheduled games reached scored outcomes, with no model-call failures or
unfinished games. Table~\ref{tab:games} retains every scheduled pairing.

\begin{description}
\item[OpenJev/Qwen.] The adapter pins
\path{mlx-community/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-4bit} at revision
\path{50d427756c6b1b2fe0c0a10f67fbda1fc8e82c1b}. It serializes the board,
recent moves and every legal candidate, then restricts one next-token forward
pass to tokenizer-verified labels. It generates no reasoning trace.
\item[ChessLFM.] The published 230M checkpoint is pinned at revision
\path{341bfd2ea4b696cda7d2016d4a721f27eb9cf34d}. Its documented protocol uses
an 80-token board representation, a predicted value token, then a legal move
token from a second forward pass~\cite{chesslfm}. The adapter omits search.
Its convolution/attention hybrid is not our learned recurrent world model.
\item[ChessFly.] Published weights are pinned at revision
\path{ed6744ec984ba401f06d6b0a966a2661b1458f53}; graph assets come from pinned
Space revision \path{375374bf58f1c828ad7d33905b64f1fa6ac4906f}.
The adapter performs five settling steps from zero per board, without the
browser demo's search. Mathematical adaptation does not establish bitwise
parity with its browser implementation. External weights and graph assets
retain their upstream terms and are not relicensed by this project.
\item[Astra via Codex.] A separately selected \texttt{gpt-6-astra} agent receives
only a board packet and legal candidates. The file bridge records request IDs,
responses, timeouts and closure. Provider identity is not attested by the bridge
itself. One persistent agent serves all puzzle and game requests, retaining
earlier packet context but receiving no gold labels or score files. This is not
an independently reset API request per move. Packets do not supply the remaining
game clocks. Isolation relies on instructions,
not an OS sandbox. It requests one move, not a fabricated probability distribution.
\end{description}

These systems differ in pretraining, parameter count, representations, search
and execution route. The text packets for Astra and Qwen also contain
SAN check and checkmate markers computed by python-chess. Three of the 24
puzzle positions have an immediate mate revealed by this notation. Numerical
policies receive legal masks but no SAN labels. This assistance is a further
confound; the panel does not measure unaided chess reasoning. Their candidate softmax scores are uncalibrated legal-move
preferences, not probabilities of winning. No engine targets are supplied to
the learned policies during play. Rules, histories and accepted moves remain
under the authoritative chess engine; policy exceptions and illegal responses
do not trigger hidden fallbacks.

The configurable protocol defaults to 300-second clocks per side, no increment,
and a 120-ply cap. A capped game is unfinished, never automatically a draw.
Automatic chess outcomes, policy/transport failures and flag falls are recorded
separately. An external request deadline shorter than the remaining game clock
can produce a failed game rather than a chess loss. Initial loading and declared
warmups are excluded from game clocks; policy wall time is charged. Astra wall
time includes dispatch, reasoning, tool use and polling. It is not comparable to
a local kernel-only inference measurement. A few games cannot estimate Elo or
reproduce a third-party tournament.

The completed public puzzle panel takes the first eight valid records per rating
band ($\leq1200$, 1201--1800, $>1800$) from a bounded official Lichess
CSV prefix~\cite{lichess}. It applies the first recorded opponent move before
scoring the first solver move. Strict gold agreement and an immediate-mate
alternative are separate metrics; neither is a full-puzzle solve rate. These
24 convenience positions are development data, not a representative benchmark
or an assuredly uncontaminated test for published models.

All 240 puzzle-policy calls completed without errors. Table~\ref{tab:puzzles}

\section{Three claims that must remain separate}
\paragraph{Computation within a position.}
The implemented recurrence refines one observed board. Resetting hidden state
between boards is part of the model. Extra internal steps are not additional
environment transitions, a search tree, or memory across moves.
\paragraph{Biological wiring.}
Our 64-unit graph is synthetic. Its sensory/inter/command/motor names do not
import a biological connectome. Separately running pretrained ChessFly cannot
isolate a causal effect of its graph; that would require common-data retraining
with biological, rewired and synthetic graph controls.
\paragraph{Recurrent world models.}
A world model requires learned, action-conditioned future-state prediction,
with appropriate multi-step evaluation; latent imagination adds another
behavior-learning question~\cite{hafner2020}. These students have neither.
Standard chess supplies an exact transition engine, so a learned world model
would additionally need to justify its utility against that available reference.

\section{Proposed memory and state-transition diagnostic}
\textbf{This section is not frozen or run, and does not rescue the failed
topology gate.} We propose testing persistent predictive state on information
absent from the current FEN. Repetition is a concrete target with an exact
reference~\cite{pythonchess}. From the standard board, repeating
\texttt{g1f3 g8f6 f3g1 f6g8} twice and playing
\texttt{g1f3 g8f6 b1c3 b8c6 c3b1 c6b8 f3g1 f6g8}
give identical full FENs, including counters, and identical final moves.
Only the first history has a third occurrence of the current position.
This rule-level construction has been checked; it is not a learned-model result.
The target is current-position threefold occurrence, not the broader possibility
of claiming a draw on a subsequent move.

Generate balanced endpoint pairs across many legal prefixes and cycle families.
Put both pair members and their whole prefix family in the same split.
Use separate training, calibration and test game-seed panels; reject cross-split
endpoint overlap while deliberately retaining identical endpoints within a
pair. Freeze a longer-cycle/distractor shift before training. Counters, previous
actions and other supplied features must match within each endpoint pair, so
labels cannot be read from an incidental shortcut. A fixed generation cap and
durable failure records prevent hand-picking successful constructions.

Compare current-only state, a persistent 64-state GRU, and that GRU with an
action-conditioned latent transition plus future-observation and repetition
heads. Add a same-sized auxiliary reconstruction control. Match feature access,
readout capacity, data, optimizer/update budgets and fresh seeds. None should
inherit pilot student weights. Exact python-chess history replay is the reference;
resetting learned state at every step is the causal memory intervention.
This isolates memory use before attempting chess-playing RL.

Predict one-, two- and four-step futures from the inferred state and supplied
actions, without later observations or future legal masks. Report exact board
accuracy, changed-square accuracy, side/castling/en-passant/counter errors,
invalid predicted states, repetition balanced accuracy, calibration and cost.
Square-average accuracy alone can reward copying unchanged pieces.
Ground-truth legality constrains data generation, not predicted rollout states.

A bounded proposed start is 4,096 training sequences, 512 calibration pairs and
512 test pairs per condition, three fresh seeds and 192 updates per arm.
Sequence lengths, loss weights, transition-loss controls, runtime budget and
precision must be frozen after generation/runtime checks that do not score the
test panel. These counts are proposals, not completed work.

A diagnostic gate should require at least 90\% repetition accuracy on both
unseen-prefix and longer-history panels in every fit, a state-reset drop of at
least 20 percentage points, and a frozen practical improvement in rollout
quality over recurrent/reconstruction controls at matched compute. The balanced
identical-input deterministic current-only control has a 50\% information ceiling; exceeding
it indicates leakage or imbalance. Account for pair/game and training-seed
variation. Passing would establish learnability and memory use, not playing
strength or novelty.

An ICLR-oriented claim still requires a specific mechanism beyond standard
recurrence/prediction, untouched transfer at matched compute, a strong explicit
associative-memory baseline, and an intervention linking mechanism to outcome.
If topology is revisited, require multiple structured and degree-preserving
random graph seeds plus common-input capacity controls. The present graph claim
remains stopped. Adaptive stopping would additionally need adaptive computation
time~\cite{graves2016}, fixed-depth, entropy-gated and matched-budget ensemble
controls, including gate cost. Conformal calibration should address a measured
decision risk rather than replace these comparisons.

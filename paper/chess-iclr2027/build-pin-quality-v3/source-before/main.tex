\begin{abstract}
We compare four structural biases in small chess decision networks: explicit
candidate consequences, biological recurrent wiring, exact computation reuse,
and action-conditioned relation transport. In a twelve-fit candidate comparison,
explicit successor differences miss predeclared engine-quality and paired-game
criteria despite sensitivity to action-successor alignment. A separate
eighteen-adapter experiment compares a fixed biological subgraph against three
degree-preserving rewires and additional recurrent controls on frozen backbones.
\BioAbstract{} A factorized relation network then separates global metadata
from local recurrence, allowing exact dependency-cone updates in real arithmetic.
Its floating-point implementation reduces logical recurrent-node updates by
\LocalSavings\%. \BatchAbstract{} \TransportAbstract{}
An eighteen-fit joint-training follow-up also fails its criterion; its advantage
over equally fine-tuned direct prediction nearly vanishes on shifted positions.
Every experiment retains its original criterion, unsuccessful arms and costs.
Together, these development results distinguish input dependence, structural
attribution, logical work, measured latency and chess strength. They do not yet
establish a novel architecture advantage. Fresh confirmation and direct
comparisons with the closest published methods remain necessary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Research question}
Chess has an exact transition simulator, a finite legal action set, strong
reference engines, and controlled interventions on input information. These
properties make it useful for testing architectural mechanisms. They also make
weak attribution easy: a legal-action mask can guarantee legal moves, a native
simulator can expose future states, and a teacher can supply strong labels
without the student learning planning.

We investigate four questions. Does explicitly encoding each candidate's
consequences improve decisions enough to justify its cost? Does biological
wiring improve a policy beyond degree-matched random wiring after sharing the
backbone, input interface, initialization and optimization? Can a representation
be designed so that different candidate consequences reuse exact computations
without invalidating the whole network? Does action-conditioned transport
through root-board relations improve a move head beyond routing and overlap
ablations or degree-preserving rewires?

The experiments form a sequential development program. Each comparison has its
own frozen protocol and panel; they are not a shared leaderboard. Results that
fail a predeclared continuation rule remain failures. Subsequent experiments
cannot retroactively convert those outcomes into successful confirmation.
The locality architecture is new code in this project, not yet a demonstrated
new contribution relative to the literature.

\section{Related work and boundaries}
Recurrent computation on chess, including extrapolation from easier to harder
problems by increasing inference iterations, is established by
\citet{schwarzschild2021}. ChessBench studies amortized chess decisions from
large-scale teacher data \citep{ruoss2024}; matching a teacher move is not the
same as winning a game. AlphaGateau studies graph representations, edge features
and policy outputs for chess \citep{rigaux2024}. Neither graph inputs nor tied
recurrence alone provides novelty here.
Probing and activation interventions have also found evidence of future-move
representations in Leela \citep{jenner2024}; exposing native successors in our
inputs does not by itself establish learned look-ahead.

FlyGM instantiates a fly connectome as a graph controller for simulated
locomotion \citep{jin2026}. ChessFly is a prior public artifact using a
FlyWire-derived graph \citep{chessfly}. Our controlled topology experiment asks
a narrower causal question than comparing heterogeneous published policies.
Our induced descending-neuron subgraph omits its original sensory inputs and
uses an artificial board interface. It is not an intact biological brain.

Incremental inference is also established. Stockfish's NNUE documentation
describes accumulator updates from sparse feature changes \citep{nnue}.
InkStream and Ripple reuse computations in evolving graph neural networks
\citep{wu2023,naman2025}. Our dependency-cone construction specializes this
principle to branching chess successors and explicitly tests global-feature
placement. It must be compared to those methods before any general efficiency
claim. TypeSafe's proprietary Jev is separate from the local research models;
none of these experiments measures its internals or reconstructs its architecture.

\section{Experimental accounting}
\paragraph{Inputs and reference targets.}
The shared spatial policy represents a board with nineteen channels and scores
all native legal UCI candidates. The channels include piece ownership, castling,
en-passant state and counters, in a side-relative rank orientation. No SAN mate
markers or engine targets are supplied to the network. Decision labels use a
2,000-node Stockfish teacher. Higher-budget assessment uses Stockfish 19 with
20,000 requested nodes for each unrestricted or chosen-move-constrained call.
For scores $c_*$ and $c_a$ in centipawns, the bounded loss is
\begin{equation}
 L(s,a)=\tanh(c_*/600)-\tanh(c_a/600).
\end{equation}
Finite-search scores can make this difference negative. It is neither exact
game-theoretic regret nor a calibrated probability. Raw centipawn loss is retained
in the underlying experiments where recorded, because the bounded transform can
hide large changes at extreme scores.

\paragraph{Panels and uncertainty.}
Ordinary and shifted rollout panels vary the generator's teacher/random-action
mix and game length. Historical roots, legal successors and color mirrors are
excluded according to each frozen plan. These are development distributions
that have already influenced architecture choices. Candidate and biological
experiments each evaluate 2,048 positions per panel, with fixed 128-position
subsets for stronger-engine grading. Within an experiment, all arms use the same
positions. Positions within games are dependent. Three fitted seeds are shown
without treating them as a large population sample.

\paragraph{Training and total cost.}
The candidate comparison uses twelve fits: four architectures and three paired
seeds, each with 32,768 training positions, six epochs and 1,536 Adam updates.
The biological study trains eighteen adapters on three frozen direct-policy
backbones with the same data count and updates. Backbone pretraining remains a
cost of the biological study; this is not evidence of low-data efficiency.
All final adapters finish before any neural evaluation. No best epoch, seed,
replacement fit or retrospective budget extension is selected.

\section{Explicit consequences do not establish better decisions}
The four candidate arms share a spatial root encoder, recurrent core and legal
candidate readout. Direct scoring uses root features only. Action-only refinement
injects source/destination/promotion features. Delta refinement also projects the
exact nineteen-channel input difference between root and native successor.
Full-afterstate refinement separately encodes the successor. The latter three
share two branch recurrence steps after the four-step root computation.
The native transitions are exact rules, not learned dynamics.

Delta improves mean bounded loss relative to direct by \DeltaDevReduction\%
on ordinary positions, but worsens it by \DeltaShiftWorsening\% on shifted
positions. Neither comparison meets the required 20\% reduction on both panels;
the action-only comparisons also fail. In 96 color-paired games per opponent,
delta scores lower-bound point shares of \DeltaDirectPoints\% against direct
and \DeltaActionPoints\% against action-only, below the required 60\%.
Two of the 288 games remain unfinished and stay in the denominators. There are
no failed games. Game scores are not converted into Elo.

Permuting which successor belongs to which candidate reduces teacher agreement
on both panels in every seed. This intervention demonstrates dependence on the
action-successor association. It does not show useful planning: that stronger
claim fails the quality and gameplay tests. Delta's complete decision latency
is about 5.5 times direct scoring in the original local measurement, including
native transition and input preparation. This cost motivates the locality
hypothesis, without assuming the existing delta method has a quality advantage
worth preserving.

\paragraph{External distribution check.}
All twelve frozen candidate models were evaluated without additional training
on a selected 4,096-position public ChessBench panel. Mean reference-move
agreement ranges from \TransferMin\% to \TransferMax\%. This external-source
measurement is development evidence; source-game identifiers are absent, and
it is not a sealed test or an independent-game uncertainty estimate. The entire
62,561-row acquired file is reserved in the later biological study's exclusions.

\section{Controlled biological topology comparison}
The audited packed graph has 138,639 nodes. A label-independent coarse-group
rule selects all 1,409 descending nodes, inducing 44,090 signed connections and
discarding 430,912 boundary connections. The selected graph contains six
isolated nodes. Original packed node indices are the available identities;
they are not asserted to be FlyWire root IDs or functional cell-type labels.

The adapter maps frozen board features into graph state with a fixed seeded
mapping, performs four leaky scalar recurrence steps, and applies a learned
residual correction through a zero-initialized output projection. State resets
on every decision. Biological wiring is compared with three independently
rewired graphs that preserve signed in/out degrees, dense recurrence, node-local
recurrence, and the unchanged backbone. Each rewire completes 440,900 accepted
swaps; structural audits also record final edge overlap. Biological and rewired
arms match parameter count and operations. Dense and node-local references do
not match those counts and are reported as additional controls.

\section{Factorized counterfactual locality}
\subsection{Architecture}
The new prototype separates local and global state. Local features comprise
twelve piece/color channels, rank/file coordinates and an en-passant marker.
Two row-normalized directed adjacency matrices encode attacks by root-side and
opponent pieces. Pinned pieces retain geometric attacks, and friendly occupied
endpoints are included. These relations are derived from chess rules, not a
biological connectome. Global metadata comprises side to move relative to the
root player, four castling rights, two counters and en-passant availability.

Let $X$ denote local features, $g$ global metadata and $A_r$ the adjacency
for relation $r$. With biases implicit, the model is
\begin{align}
 z &= X E, & h^0 &= \tanh(z),\\
 h^{t+1} &= \tfrac12 h^t + \tfrac12\tanh\!\left(h^tW_0+
                   \sum_r A_rh^tW_r+z\right),\\
 q(s) &= \mathrm{MLP}\left[\mathrm{mean}(h^T),g\right].
\end{align}
Node features are row vectors. A broadcast control
adds the same projected global vector $gG$ to every row of $z$, including initialization. Global changes
therefore invalidate all nodes in that control. In the factorized model they
affect the readout immediately but need not invalidate local recurrence.
The functions differ across architectures; only full and incremental evaluation
of the \emph{same} architecture must agree. Late fusion may also reduce modeling
capacity, which remains to be measured after matched training.

\subsection{Exact dependency-cone updates}
Cache root states $h^0,\ldots,h^T$ once. For a native successor in the same
root-player perspective, let $S_0$ contain changed local input nodes and $D$
contain destinations with changed normalized adjacency rows. Recursively define
\begin{equation}
 S_{t+1}=S_t\cup S_0\cup D\cup\{v: \exists u\in S_t,r,\ A'_{r,vu}\ne0\}.
\end{equation}
Recompute layer $t+1$ only at destinations in $S_{t+1}$, using the updated
predecessor layer, and copy other nodes from root layer $t+1$.

\paragraph{Equivalence.}
Assume fixed parameters, deterministic node-local nonlinearities, and no hidden
global normalization or pooling inside recurrence. At initialization, unchanged
input nodes have identical states. Suppose all child states outside $S_t$ equal
their root states. For $v$ outside $S_{t+1}$, the local input, self state and
normalized adjacency rows are unchanged, and no child edge reaches $v$ from a
changed predecessor. Thus its entire update equals the root update. Induction
proves equality at every layer in real arithmetic. Deleted edges and changed
normalizers are included in $D$, preventing incorrect stale-cache reuse.
This is an application of incremental-computation reasoning, not a claim of a
new general theorem. Floating-point implementations require tolerance checks.

\subsection{Engineering screen}
Before inspecting outcomes, the screen fixed 64 old training roots in a
label-independent hash order, all legal successors, widths of 32, depths
2/4/8, three initialization seeds, and factorized/broadcast arms. No optimizer,
engine or external model is called. The screen checks every successor's hidden
state and scalar score against full recomputation with absolute tolerance
$2\times10^{-6}$. A stale-root diagnostic distinguishes correct updating from
doing no work.

The logical work comparison charges root-cache construction as well as all
child node updates. Complete timing uses sixteen roots, three paired repeats,
depth four and seed 211, alternating method order. It includes FEN parsing,
legal moves, native board copies, graph construction, root caching where needed,
propagation and readout. The current dense masks and tensor clones remain in
the timed path. Fewer logical updates are not assumed to imply lower latency.
The initial full baseline processes one candidate at a time. Its original gate
remains unchanged after the following stronger baseline comparison.

\section{What remains before the main claim}
The experiments delimit failure modes and provide executable hypotheses, not a
general quality-cost frontier. A decisive next study must identify a mechanism
that survives the closest published-method comparators and task-specific
interventions. The completed graph comparisons favor the established WLDN
adaptation over scalar contrast (Appendix~\ref{app:graph-baselines}); child-assignment
sensitivity is insufficient. Controls need
explicit information, training and inference budgets. Efficiency claims also
require suitable NNUE and incremental-GNN baselines. Architecture choices must
be frozen before a disjoint confirmation panel is exposed.

A further hypothesis is a learned board-to-connectome mapping compared to
identically learned mappings on several degree-preserving rewires. It is not
implemented as a validated improvement here. Combining the tested transport,
locality and biological modules would require factorial ablations showing that
each contributes beyond the others.

Longer-term claims need strong engine-quality and paired-game evidence with
source-game-aware uncertainty, plus a second relational task if the claim is
intended to generalize beyond chess. A successful engineering screen alone
cannot meet those requirements. Human scientific review, complete citation
verification, and an anonymity audit remain prerequisites to submission.

\section{Earlier development experiments}
The initial synthetic circuit had mean teacher agreement of 14.36\%, versus
14.19\% for a signed degree-preserving rewire, below its required three-point
advantage. A subsequent spatial study compared untied convolution, tied
recurrence, current-board reconstruction and future-piece prediction. Prediction
learned changed-square targets but failed the prescribed decision-quality gain
over the controls. Mixed-depth and anchored recurrence were examined separately;
they did not establish the intended robust extra-computation benefit.

A six-fit width comparison increased active parameters from 33,185 to 439,073.
Its mean bounded engine loss improved by 22.99\% on ordinary positions and
9.26\% on shifted positions, missing the required 20\% improvement on both.
Against the small model it scored 18 wins, 64 draws, 12 losses and two unfinished
games, below the required lower-bound 60\% point share. These experiments used
different panels and budgets from the main tables and must not be pooled into
a single benchmark or used as independent replications.
